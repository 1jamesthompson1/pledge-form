## Waldorf Curriculum — Schooling the Hands, Heart and Head

- Education of the whole child: head, heart, hands
- Curriculum based on child development
- The unhurried curriculum — readiness for learning
- Integrated journey from playgroup through primary school
- Teachers strive to educate all aspects of the child in a healthy, unhurried manner
- Aims to integrate art, science, and spirituality
- Celebrates Christian festivals, Matariki, and other cultural/seasonal festivals (no religious instruction)
- Draws on a century of Waldorf practice in a contemporary Aotearoa context

### Three Phases of Childhood

- **0–7 years**: Learn through imitation and play — essential goodness
- **7–14 years**: Curriculum inspires wonder at beauty around them
- **14–21 years**: Guided toward independent thinking — find their own truth

## Waldorf Education — From Steiner's Vision to Te Ra's Unique Approach

- Inspired by Rudolf Steiner (1861–1925), based on anthroposophy
- "Waldorf" name from Waldorf Astoria factory in Stuttgart, Germany
- Also referred to as "Steiner Schools"
- Te Ra's curriculum embedded in Aotearoa New Zealand context
- Exploring anthroposophy beyond pedagogical principles is a matter of personal choice
- Practical applications: curative homes (Hohepa), medicine (Dr Hauschka, Weleda), performing arts (Eurythmy), agriculture (biodynamics/Demeter)

**Whāngaia te wairua o te tamaiti** — nourish the soul of each child
**Hāpainga te mana tangata** — uphold their dignity
**Poipoia te mauri** — nurture their life force
